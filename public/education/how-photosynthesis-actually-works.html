How photosynthesis actually works, step by step

By Priya Nathan Published Sep 24, 2026 8 min read

Every biology class covers photosynthesis, but most rush past the part that actually matters: how a plant turns light into a stable, storable form of energy. Here's the process broken into the four steps that make it click.

In this article

  • 1. Capturing light
  • 2. Splitting water
  • 3. Fixing carbon
  • 4. Why it matters beyond biology class

1. Capturing light

Chlorophyll molecules inside a leaf's chloroplasts absorb red and blue wavelengths of sunlight and reflect green — which is why leaves look green to us. That absorbed energy excites electrons, kicking off the whole process.

2. Splitting water

The plant uses that excited-electron energy to split water molecules into oxygen, protons, and electrons. The oxygen is released into the air — it's the oxygen we breathe. The electrons head into an energy-carrying chain.

The oxygen you're breathing right now was very likely released by a plant splitting water molecules apart, sometime in the last few weeks.

3. Fixing carbon

In the second stage, called the Calvin cycle, the plant uses the energy carriers made from steps one and two to grab carbon dioxide from the air and stitch it into glucose — a stable sugar molecule the plant can use immediately or store for later.

4. Why it matters beyond biology class

Photosynthesis is the base of almost every food chain on Earth and the reason the atmosphere has enough oxygen to support animal life at all. Understanding the mechanism — not just the word — makes topics like climate change, crop science, and even solar-fuel research much easier to follow later.
